# Australian Food Business Compliance Checklist — Diwaan Shah Khwaan

> This is a practical checklist, **not legal or tax advice**. Requirements change, and they depend on facts only the
> owner knows. Confirm each item with the City of Gosnells Environmental Health team, the ATO and/or a registered
> tax agent before launch. Sources are listed at the end (checked 5 November 2026).

Pickup location: **Langford WA 6147**, which falls within the **City of Gosnells** local government area.

## 1. Food business notification / registration (City of Gosnells, Food Act 2008 (WA))

- [ ] **Before selling any food**, notify or register the food business with the City of Gosnells (Environmental
      Health Services). Under the Food Act 2008 (WA) and Food Regulations 2009 (WA) this is required "before selling
      or producing food intended for sale".
- [ ] Ask the City specifically about **home-based food businesses**: whether your home kitchen can be registered,
      and what fit-out, inspection or conditions apply.
- [ ] Check whether a **home-based business / home occupation (planning) approval** is also needed from the City.
- [ ] Budget for the fees. City of Gosnells 2025–26 fees: notification $67; registration $217 (new premises) or $79
      (existing); annual risk-based inspection of $262–$528 depending on risk classification. Confirm the current fees.
- [ ] Keep the registration certificate and any conditions on file. Update the City if details change.
- Contact: City of Gosnells, 2120 Albany Highway, Gosnells WA 6110 · (08) 9397 3021 · council@gosnells.wa.gov.au

## 2. Food Safety Standard 3.2.2A (in force since 8 December 2023)

Takeaway and catering businesses that make unpackaged, potentially hazardous ready-to-eat food are generally
**Category one**. Confirm your category with the City.

- [ ] **Food Safety Supervisor (FSS):** appoint a certified FSS (trained through a registered training organisation),
      who is reasonably available while food is handled. Renew the certificate every 5 years.
- [ ] **Food handler training:** everyone handling unpackaged, potentially hazardous food completes food safety
      training (for example a free course accepted in WA) or can show equivalent knowledge.
- [ ] **Substantiation (Category one):** keep records of key controls (e.g. cooking, cooling and hot-holding
      temperatures, cleaning and sanitising) for at least 3 months, or be able to demonstrate safe practices.
- [ ] Also follow Standards 3.2.2 (Food Safety Practices) and 3.2.3 (Premises and Equipment).

The dashboard's **Kitchen schedule** (printable) helps with daily production planning. It doesn't replace the
temperature and cleaning records required by 3.2.2A.

## 3. Allergens and food information

- [ ] For every dish, record ingredients and declarable allergens. The 2024 Food Standards Code list is: peanut, tree
      nuts, milk, egg, sesame, fish, crustacea, mollusc, soy, lupin, wheat, gluten (barley, oats, rye, wheat),
      sulphites ≥10 mg/kg. Check suppliers' labels for sauces, spice mixes and breads.
- [ ] In **Admin → Menu → Edit dish**, tick "I've checked this dish's allergens" only after verifying. Until then the
      website clearly says allergens are being confirmed and asks allergy sufferers to call.
- [ ] Be able to answer allergen questions accurately on the phone, and keep the website consistent with the recipes.
- [ ] Confirm with the City what allergen information must be displayed or provided for unpackaged food sold for
      pickup. The national Food Standards Code currently has no separate rule for online menus.
- [ ] Food is collected **hot and packaged**. Include "eat promptly or refrigerate within 2 hours" guidance (already
      in the Terms) and consider a label with the business name, date and allergens on each container.

## 4. Business identity, tax and receipts

- [ ] **ABN:** apply for or confirm your ABN. Enter it in Admin → Settings. The ATO checksum is validated.
- [ ] **GST registration:** registration is required once GST turnover reaches **$75,000** a year (voluntary below
      that). Ask your tax agent.
- [ ] **GST treatment by dish:** each dish has a Taxable / GST-free setting. ATO guidance: hot takeaway food,
      prepared meals and food for consumption on the premises are generally **taxable**. Some cold items (certain
      desserts, dips) need checking against the ATO detailed food list or food and beverage search tool. All dishes
      are seeded as **Taxable** pending confirmation.
- [ ] Only switch on **GST registered** in Settings once registered. The app then issues **tax invoices** (ABN, GST
      amount, taxable items marked). Until then it issues receipts stating no GST was charged.
- [ ] Keep financial records for 5 years. Use the Reports CSV export for your bookkeeper.

## 5. Consumer law and payments

- [ ] Refund and cancellation policy reviewed. It must not limit **Australian Consumer Law** guarantees (the template
      says so explicitly).
- [ ] Prices shown are the total price payable (no hidden surcharges). If you ever add a card surcharge, it must not
      exceed your cost of acceptance and must be disclosed.
- [ ] **Stripe account** in the business's name, verified. Test thoroughly in test mode. **Live payments require
      explicit owner approval** (`ALLOW_LIVE_PAYMENTS=true` together with live keys).
- [ ] No card data is stored by this application (Stripe-hosted checkout).

## 6. Privacy

- [ ] Review the privacy policy template. Small businesses under $3 million turnover are generally exempt from the
      Privacy Act, but following the Australian Privacy Principles is good practice and expected by customers.
- [ ] Limit who has staff accounts. Use the Kitchen role for helpers. Review the audit log periodically.
- [ ] Email and SMS providers may store data overseas. This is disclosed in the policy; confirm your providers.
- [ ] Only send marketing to customers who opt in (no marketing is sent by default).

## 7. Insurance and other

- [ ] Public and product liability insurance covering home-based food production and sales.
- [ ] Check home and contents insurance and any rental, strata or lease restrictions on running a business from home.
- [ ] If you do bulk or BBQ catering at other venues, ask the City about any extra approvals (e.g. temporary or mobile
      food business, event stallholder permits).
